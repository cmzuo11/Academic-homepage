About

Chunman Zuo is an Associate Professor and PhD Supervisor in the Department of Bioinformatics, School of Life Sciences, Sun Yat-sen University, and a Rising Scholar under the university's Yat-sen Scholar Program. She received her PhD in Computer Science from Jilin University, undertook joint doctoral training in biostatistics/bioinformatics at the University of Georgia, USA, and completed postdoctoral research at the Shanghai Institute of Biochemistry and Cell Biology, Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Her research spans artificial intelligence, data science, generative AI, and multimodal learning, with a focus on virtual organs, dynamic network biology, and integrative modeling of medical imaging, biological networks, and multi-omics data. A central objective is to identify key factors driving early cancer development and support early diagnosis and intervention.

Research Interests

We develop AI and statistical methods to build virtual organs and tissues, characterize early tumor-associated changes, and predict key drivers of disease initiation, with the goal of supporting disease prevention and early diagnosis.

1. 3D/4D Spatiotemporal Omics Integration and Visualization

Develop original computational tools for integrating, mining, and visualizing single-cell and spatial multi-omics data—including transcriptomic, metabolomic, genomic, methylation, and chromatin accessibility measurements—together with biological networks. These methods aim to quantify tumor microenvironment heterogeneity, clinical characteristics, and regulatory mechanisms.

2. Multimodal Biomedical Data Modeling

Design AI methods to integrate heterogeneous data across scales, including medical images, clinical records, biological sequences, and high-throughput molecular measurements, to improve disease prediction, identify biomarkers, and construct patient-specific networks for intelligent healthcare.

3. Computational Methods for Clinical Applications

Integrate diverse datasets to identify and interpret critical states across disease progression, including gallbladder, gastric, colorectal, and esophageal cancers, and inform strategies for early diagnosis and treatment.

Postdoctoral Researchers

Applications are welcome from candidates in bioinformatics and related fields. Applicants should:

  • Hold or be close to completing a PhD in computer science, applied statistics, mathematics, bioinformatics, or a related discipline.
  • Demonstrate independent thinking, self-motivation, and strong teamwork skills.
  • Preferably have published or accepted research in high-quality international journals.

The group currently includes two postdoctoral researchers. Undergraduate students interested in thesis projects are also welcome; undergraduate first authors from the group have published in journals including National Science Review, Genomics, Proteomics & Bioinformatics, Briefings in Bioinformatics, and Cell Reports Methods.

“Give someone a fish and you feed them for a day; teach someone to fish and you feed them for a lifetime.” The lab emphasizes creativity, interdisciplinary perspectives, personal development, and scientific independence. Monthly living allowances and additional rewards for significant research achievements are available to lab members.
